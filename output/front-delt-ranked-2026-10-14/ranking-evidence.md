# 7 Front-Delt Exercises, Ranked

Project: http://127.0.0.1:3000/?job=bbc9ab47-3ea8-48b3-8b7a-102f82ae5a0d
Audience: general gym lifters choosing front-delt exercises for muscle-building, rather than competing in handstand or overhead-press skills.

## How the ranking was decided

This is an evidence-informed editorial ranking. No study directly establishes this order across the seven exercises. We weigh useful front-delt loading and progression, skill/setup demands, and control when a repetition fails. An exercise can rank highly despite technical demands when its training advantages justify learning it. No arbitrary numerical injury-risk scores or fabricated injury rates are used.

| Rank | Exercise | Benefit kept in the ranking | Tradeoff and reason for this placement | Evidence / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Machine Shoulder Press | Back support, guided pressing and simple load adjustments can make hard sets repeatable. | First for this audience when seat, handles and path fit the lifter. A poorly fitting machine loses that advantage. | Lower stabilization demand is described in [A]; final placement is practical judgment, not superior growth proof. |
| 2 | Dumbbell Shoulder Press | Direct front-delt loading, independent arms and a flexible path. | Strong general option; getting heavy dumbbells into position requires control. | Anterior-delt involvement in [B] and [C]; setup tradeoff is mechanical reasoning. |
| 3 | Barbell Overhead Press | Familiar, measurable loading progression and overhead pressing strength. | Bracing and bar control demand learning, but those demands do not erase its value. | Technique, bracing, load control and failed-attempt handling in [D]. | 4 | Smith Shoulder Press | Guided bar path reduces balancing demands. | Worth ranking higher for an individual when the path fits; bench positioning and a fixed path make fit less adaptable than independent dumbbells. | Guided vs. freely chosen paths in [A]; Smith-specific placement is inference, not a Smith vs. seven-exercise trial. |
| 5 | Pike Push-Up | Shoulder-focused bodyweight pressing with no external weights. | Valuable without equipment; form and body position matter, and adding tiny load increments is less direct than changing external weight. | Shoulder/anterior-delt emphasis and form in [E]; progression comparison is mechanical reasoning. |

## Sources and limits

[A] Haugen et al. 2023, systematic review/meta-analysis: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-023-00713-4
No significant hypertrophy difference detected between free weights and machines in the included studies; this is not proof that every individual exercise or person responds identically. Strength gains depend on the tested modality. The paper explicitly describes uncertainty about causal differences in injury risk, so we do not label free weights inherently dangerous or machines inherently safe.

[B] ACE shoulder study, 2014: https://www.acefitness.org/certifiednews/images/article/pdfs/ACEShoulderStudy.pdf
Acute EMG in 16 men supports the front-delt roles of shoulder pressing and front raises. It is not a longitudinal growth comparison and is not used to translate higher EMG into higher growth.

[C] Saeterbakken & Fimland 2013: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23096062/
Fifteen men tested seated/standing barbell/dumbbell shoulder presses using EMG and 1RM. Loading modality and stabilization affect activity and strength. These acute results do not prove that dumbbells grow front delts more than barbells.

[D] CSCCa Barbell Overhead Press: https://cscca.org/content.aspx?club_id=408309&module_id=732261&page_id=22
Professional technique guidance for rigid torso, bracing, bar position, lowering and handling a failed attempt. Guidance is not an injury-incidence experiment.

[E] NASM Pike Push-Up: https://www.nasm.org/resource-center/exercise-library/pike-push-up
Used for anterior-deltoid emphasis, high hips and controlled lowering. Did not copy its broader promises about athletic benefits.

[F] Johnson et al. 2019, author-university record: https://scholars.georgiasouthern.edu/en/publications/exercise-technique-handstand-push-up/
Peer-reviewed technique article abstract describes strength, balance and stabilization relevance. Full publisher text was inaccessible, so no detailed clinical or comparative injury claims were drawn from it.
